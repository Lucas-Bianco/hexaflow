    Technical report 01 · Tri-County RVTHS · NASA HUNCH

    Growing plants in microgravity, the way a thorny devil drinks

    Hexagonal capillary channels, copied from a lizard, carry water to plant roots where gravity cannot help. This is the record of how five students designed, dropped and measured them.

    ProjectHexaFlow, modular capillary nanolab
    TeamAidan, Nate, Sabrina, Maddie, Lucas
    SchoolTri-County RVTHS, Franklin, Massachusetts
    ProgramNASA HUNCH
    Tested atNASA Glenn 2.2-Second Drop Tower, Ohio

    1Summary

    HexaFlow is a modular capillary action nanolab that uses AI vision and biomimicry to deliver water to plants. A five-member team from Tri-County RVTHS built it inside a 2U enclosure, on a $100 budget, to prevent root rot while enabling interchangeable experiments and rapid data collection.

    Note2U envelope is 20 × 10 × 10 cm. Power is 5 V USB-A.

    The system was tested in a 2.2-second free-fall window at NASA Glenn’s 2.2-Second Drop Tower in Ohio. Manufacturing relies on SLA 3D printing in resin. Resin’s polar bonds grip water more tightly than water molecules grip each other, driving efficient capillary transport, strong enough that the system holds water even when inverted.

    2Capillary channels

    2.1The thorny devil

    The hexagonal channels take their cue from the thorny devil lizard (Moloch horridus), which transports water through grooved skin by capillary action, no pump required. A vision model reads root moisture from the camera and drives the pump, closing a continuous feedback loop that prevents overwatering and root rot.

    OriginSabrina found the lizard on asknature.org. Nate prototyped the “Moloch Horridus Water System” in SolidWorks in two sizes, 22 Oct 2025.

    At Preliminary Design Review, NASA’s reviewer called the hexagonal shape and thorny-devil method “terrific.” Scroll through Fig. 2 to watch the channel network drawn in, then filled from the water supply upward.

    2.2Channel network

    Channel width is 0.48 mm (a 0.020 in gap). A fully saturated lattice holds 10.12 mL. Every channel is SLA-printed resin.

    3Measured data

    The headline numbers come from the local 14-foot rig, where the team could run enough drops to build a baseline. Mean capillary movement rose from roughly 9.9 mm in the first suite to 14.7 mm after the module was revised.

    NoteThe spread across the nine revised-suite drops ran from 4.28 mm to 31.79 mm. Per-drop data is held with the CDR test documentation.

    5Design journey, selected entries

    • Ideation22 Oct 2025

      From hydroponics to a thorny devil

      Eight substrates went into testing in October. Nearly all of them grew mould; only pine bark produced any growth at all. On 22 October the team abandoned growing media entirely and went aeroponic.

    • Design18–19 Nov 2025

      NASA liked the hex. Then pushed us.

      At Preliminary Design Review, NASA’s reviewer called the hexagonal shape and thorny-devil method “terrific.” The feedback that reshaped the build: reposition the camera to capture fluid dynamics, not plant growth, and use food coloring to see the water.

    • TestJan–Mar 2026

      Drop, measure, repeat, nine times

      We dropped the TDC from 14 feet, with dyed water, measuring the water radius before and after each drop, about 0.9 seconds of free-fall per drop. Two suites were run: six drops first, then nine more after the module was revised, averaging 14.7 mm.
